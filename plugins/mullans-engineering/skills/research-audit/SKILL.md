# Research audit

Assess whether research is producing supported knowledge, reducing consequential
uncertainty and advancing the user's goals. Use existing evidence; identify gaps
rather than starting new experiments or building measurement infrastructure as
part of the audit.

## Set scope

Identify the requested assessment: knowledge or capability progress, process
efficiency, priorities or a combination. Use the project's audit guide, when
present, for relevant areas, source entry points and report placement.

State included areas and material exclusions. Select a relevant prior audit or
comparison state and interval when judging change. If that comparison is needed
but unavailable, qualify the judgment. A focused process question need not
manufacture a baseline or review the whole portfolio.

Use maintained references within their stated scope. Retrieve outcomes and
evidence relevant to the assessment. Consult original evidence for contradictions,
consequential uncertainty, missing detail or substantive claim changes.
Continuation records help locate unfinished work; they are not a complete
project-state index.

## Assess evidence and progress

When comparing progress, a table can help:

| Area | Baseline | Current supported knowledge or capability | Change or blocker | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

For a narrow process review, use concrete cases and a supported judgment instead
of constructing a capability inventory. State what the evidence establishes, its
strength, relevant conditions and limits. Avoid completion percentages for unknown
requirements. Copies and summaries of one result are not independent corroboration.

Review material outcomes, failures, regressions and expensive or stalled work
within scope. Sample routine work when needed to assess a pattern; identify the
sample and material gaps. Expand only when another case can change the judgment.

## Assess decisions and effort

Judge choices against the information available at the time. Supported negative
results can be complete research contributions. Select applicable questions from
this table rather than treating every row as a required assessment:

| Area | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Reuse | Were applicable findings used, and repeated questions justified by changed conditions? |
| Method | Could existing evidence or a simpler method have answered the question? |
| Human effort | Did preparation and supported automation reduce avoidable setup, waiting and repeated actions? |
| Scope and stopping | Did work reach a useful answer or explain the unresolved question without continuing low-value investigation? |
| Priorities | Did effort address the user's goals and consequential dependencies? |
| Reporting | Do reported findings, capabilities and pending work reflect supported outcomes and limits? |

Support material criticism with a concrete case and consequence. Distinguish an
isolated failure from a recurring pattern and identify effective practices worth
retaining. Assess prior adopted recommendations when relevant: helped, failed,
untested or no longer applicable.

Use available effort records. Distinguish measurements, estimates and unknowns;
compare equivalent scopes and separate external waits from active effort. Do not
claim precision unsupported by the data or reward omission of necessary checks.

## Report and recommend

Return the requested assessment or update its existing owner. Create a standalone
audit report when requested or useful for durable comparison, not merely because
the skill ran. Include only the relevant elements:

- Scope, comparison basis where needed, inspected coverage and material gaps.
- Supported progress judgments or source-linked process findings.
- Recommendations, their value and consequential dependencies.
- Assessment of relevant prior recommendations.

No change recommended is a valid outcome. Rank alternatives only when
prioritization is relevant and evidence supports an ordering; otherwise state the
uncertainty or dependency rather than inventing a global priority list. Reference
limits do not automatically become test commitments.

Use tables and narrative where they clarify the judgment. Add acceptance or
reopening conditions only when useful for the decision.

Finish when the scoped questions have been assessed or explain the evidence
preventing a conclusion. Identify needed evidence as future work. Recommendations
do not authorize implementation or reopening settled documents; apply only changes
already within authorization and check affected navigation when it changes.

Documentation organization belongs to `maintain-research-docs`. Promotion of
supported knowledge and methods from current work or explicitly selected
investigations belongs to `research-promotion`.
